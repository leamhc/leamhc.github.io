There are big banks, and then there are really really big banks. 

The latter are known as global systemically important banks (G-Sibs) - financial institutions whose failure could trigger a global financial crisis.

The Basel Committee on Banking Supervision’s assessment methodology, which evaluates systemic risk based on 14 indicators grouped spread across categories. 

Each bank’s score is calculated by dividing its indicator values, denominated in euros, by the aggregate amount for each indicator across all banks in the G-Sib sample. This comprises the 75 largest global banks by leverage exposure, banks already designated as G-Sibs the previous year and any other bank added by national supervisors. The result is multiplied by 10,000 and averaged across the five categories, with the substitutability score capped at 500bp.

This interactive treemap animation shows how the G-Sib scores of the US bank giants have changed over time. The size of each rectangle represents the banks' aggregate G-Sib score for that year, while the colour indicates the categories.
